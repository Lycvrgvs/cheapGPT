The step back: one method at three sizes (tiny: you, a few lines; full: a fresh loop-breaker, a few paragraphs; re-plan: the full step back with the user, who approves before anything is built).
- The whole before the detail: restate the goal and the targets' literal criteria; read all the evidence (every run, finding, review and dropped attempt), not only the latest.
- Name the class of failure and the root cause it shares, in the problem, never in the last fix. A fix justified only by an earlier fix is a spiral.
- Removal first: what can be deleted or simplified instead of added?
- The real property, not a look-alike: does the evidence show the property, or something that passes without it?
- Then one correction at the origin, stated as the rule for the whole property, and its smallest coherent move.
- Tiny also: reproduce on BASE a target that already failed, test the named cause with its cheapest discriminating check, and allocate (the dynamic/mechanical line).
- Full and re-plan also: name the step at fault (brief, mechanism, plan or structure).
