The step back: look at the whole before acting. It is the first half of every prevent; the one-block follows it.
- Sizes. Tiny: a few lines, by you (adoption, a FOCUSED plan, every tiny loop, the release gate). Full: a page, by the planner (a FULL plan) or a fresh loop-breaker, from scratch from the plan and the evidence, not from the failing attempt. Re-plan: the full step back on the user's comment.
- The whole before the detail: restate the goal and the targets' literal acceptance lines; read all the evidence (every run, finding, review and dropped attempt), not only the latest.
- Name the class of failure and the root cause it shares, in the problem, never in the last fix. A fix justified only by an earlier fix is a spiral.
- Removal first: what can be deleted or simplified instead of added?
- The real property, not a look-alike: does the evidence show the property, or something that passes without it?
- Then the one risk, and one correction at the origin, stated as the rule for the whole property.
- In a tiny loop also: reproduce on BASE a target that already failed, test the named cause with its cheapest discriminating check, and allocate (the dynamic/mechanical line). Full and re-plan also: name the step at fault (brief, mechanism, plan or structure).
